Looking for free WiFi? You just scanned a random QR code.

There is no free WiFi here, and this time nothing bad happened. A real attacker could have used this same trick to steal your passwords.

This was an awareness demo. Nothing was collected from your phone.

How this trick works

Attackers use tempting labels like "Free WiFi", "Free Data", or "Scan to win" to get people to scan without second thought.

Once scanned, you are redirected to a convincing replica of a login page (such as student portals, email providers, or social accounts) that silently logs your credentials the moment you submit them.

This page does not provide WiFi and is not connected to any real network. It is a simulated teaching demo for campus awareness.
Risks

What could have happened

When a malicious QR code is scanned, an attacker can trigger several immediate threats:

A fake login page stealing your passwords

You land on a spoofed student portal or email login built to harvest your username, index number, and password.

A malicious file download

The link automatically initiates a download of a harmful file or APK that can spy on your device or access private data.

A scam payment page

You are directed to an illegitimate payment prompt requesting Mobile Money authorisations or debit card details under false pretences.

Prevention

How to scan safely

Five practical habits to protect your accounts and devices:

  1. 01

    Preview the link before opening it

    Modern camera apps display a preview of the destination URL. Verify the exact domain before tapping. Look out for altered spellings such as knust-login.com instead of knust.edu.gh.

  2. 02

    Never enter passwords or payment details after a scan

    Legitimate public flyers rarely require entering passwords, MoMo PINs, or card numbers immediately after scanning. If an unexpected prompt appears, close it.

  3. 03

    Check whether a sticker has been placed over another QR code

    Attackers frequently paste their own printed stickers directly on top of genuine notices, cafeteria tables, or posters. Inspect for peeling edges or raised tape before scanning.

  4. 04

    Be suspicious of QR codes promising free data, prizes, or jobs

    Offers that seem too generous exist to bypass your caution. Unsolicited promises of cash stipends or free internet packages are common bait used in credential phishing.

  5. 05

    When in doubt, type the official website yourself

    If a flyer references a university announcement, registration, or service, navigate to the verified portal manually in your browser rather than relying on a printed barcode.

Think Before You Click

Stay Safe in the Digital World

Cybersecurity starts with one conscious pause. Share these five habits with colleagues, roommates, and study groups to help keep our campus safe.
